# Narrative and source model

The primary path contains 21 chapters in the three parts requested in the brief. Each chapter introduces the situation, follows a turning point, and ends with a consequence leading into the next chapter. Sentences use contemporary English. All prose is a retelling; none is presented as a quotation from Homer.

Chapter 17 is explicitly a look back at events in Ithaca during Odysseus's absence. It is placed immediately before his disguised return so new readers understand what he faces at home.

The optional Original epic order is a teaching route through the frame narrative: Ithaca (Books 1–4), Calypso (5), the Phaeacians (6–8), narrated adventures (9–12), and the return (13–24). The interface labels the flashback chapters. It groups episodes and does not reproduce every change of viewpoint or repeated event. The Trojan War prelude remains outside this mode.

Each chapter defines its identifier, number, part, title, location, introduction, characters, scene artwork, narrative beats, optional objects, source label/link, previous/next identifiers and completion condition. Completion occurs when the final beat's next/complete control is used. Optional exploration is never required.

## Source distinctions

- Chapters 1–3 use later mythic background in Apollodorus's Library/Epitome, with parallel traditions acknowledged.
- Chapter 4 explains the Iliad's limited wartime span and its ending at Hector's funeral.
- Chapter 5 uses the Odyssey's recollection of the horse and clearly distinguishes the wider Trojan War tradition.
- Chapters 6–21 follow the Odyssey, with book references visible at each chapter.
- Helen's agency, the modern location of mythic islands, and the precise relationship of Homeric Ithaca to modern geography are qualified.
- The underworld scene depicts the summoning of shades at the edge of Ocean. The boulder, Nobody name, heated stake, sheep escape and boast in the Cyclops chapter follow Odyssey 9.

Source links are stored with the content, displayed in story and character views, and collected in About the epic. The principal reference is Theoi's Classical Texts Library (public-domain translations by A. T. Murray and J. G. Frazer). This edition is an introduction to the narrative, not an archaeological claim or a critical translation.

The browser suite walks every beat and chapter, checks chapter order and completion persistence, and proves that the entire primary narrative can be finished without visiting the atlas or character explorer. Character entries are reachable from their chapters and link back into the story.
